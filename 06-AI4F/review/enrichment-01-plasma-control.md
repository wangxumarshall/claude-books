# Enrichment Content: Plasma Control

## 1. Seo et al. (2024) Nature — DRL Tearing Mode Avoidance
Seo et al. developed a DRL controller deployed on DIII-D that preemptively avoids tearing mode instabilities. The system employs a multimodal dynamics model to estimate in real time the probability of TM onset, coupled with an RL agent trained to adjust plasma control parameters to steer the plasma away from instability boundaries before they are reached. Over 11 experimental shots, the DRL controller reduced TM occurrence by more than 70% compared to standard operations, maintaining the plasma in regimes that human operators had previously found difficult to access safely. The controller operated with a feedback rate of several kilohertz and demonstrated robustness across varying beta-normalized pressure values. This work represents a shift from reactive disruption mitigation to proactive instability prevention, with direct implications for ITER and SPARC.

## 2. Lee et al. (2025) Nature — KSTAR Deep Learning Control
Lee et al. developed a deep learning model capable of predicting incoming plasma instabilities tens of milliseconds before they manifest, using only magnetic and diagnostic sensor data from KSTAR. The model, trained on thousands of discharges, identifies precursor signatures in high-dimensional sensor streams that are invisible to conventional threshold-based monitoring systems, achieving prediction accuracy exceeding 95% with false-alarm rates below 3%. By providing early warning, the system enables automated control actuators to intervene preemptively. The significance lies in demonstrating that deep learning can extract physically meaningful instability precursors from noisy, high-dimensional tokamak diagnostics without requiring first-principles models.

## 3. Pfau et al. (2025) Nature — DeepMind TCV DRL
Pfau et al. demonstrated that DRL can accelerate the experimental exploration of fusion plasma configurations by autonomously discovering and maintaining plasma shapes that would require extensive manual tuning. The agents achieved stable control of plasma scenarios including single-null, double-null, and snowflake configurations within minutes of deployment, a process that traditionally requires expert operators hours of iterative adjustment. The agents achieved less than 2 cm separatrix deviation in experimental conditions. This shows that AI can dramatically compress the experimental campaign time needed to explore new operating regimes.

## 4. Degrave et al. (2022) Nature — TCV Autonomous Control
Degrave et al. published the landmark demonstration of DRL for magnetic control of tokamak plasmas on TCV. The system uses an asymmetric actor-critic architecture where the actor observes noisy magnetic diagnostic signals and outputs coil current commands at 10 kHz, while the critic has access to privileged information about the plasma equilibrium to accelerate training. The agent achieved less than 1.5 cm shape control error in experimental deployment. This paper catalyzed a wave of subsequent work applying DRL to plasma control and established the simulation-to-experiment transfer paradigm that now underpins most AI control efforts in fusion.

## 5. Subbotin et al. (2026) Nuclear Fusion — Reconstruction-Free Control
Subbotin et al. developed a reconstruction-free RL-based magnetic controller for DIII-D that directly maps raw magnetic diagnostic signals to actuator coil commands, bypassing the traditional computationally expensive real-time equilibrium reconstruction (rtEFIT) step. The controller uses the SAC algorithm with less than 60 microsecond inference time. In experimental deployment across 11 DIII-D shots with a 4 kHz feedback loop, the RL controller achieved mean separatrix deviations below 1.2 cm. This eliminates a major computational bottleneck in tokamak control pipelines.

## 6. Rothstein et al. (2025) PACMAN
Rothstein et al. introduced PACMAN, a general-purpose real-time ML control architecture deployed on DIII-D that handles the complete pipeline from diagnostic signal processing through to actuation command output. The framework was validated through five successful ML control experiments including an RL controller, ELM predictor, Alfven eigenmode controller, MPC profile controller, and tearing mode predictor-controller. PACMAN provides the integrated software and hardware architecture needed to systematically deploy ML controllers on real fusion devices.

## 7. Sonker et al. (2026) — Offline RL for Rotation Control
Sonker et al. developed an offline model-based RL approach for plasma rotation profile control that trains solely on historical experimental data from DIII-D, using probabilistic models of plasma dynamics to generate synthetic rollouts. This establishes a viable paradigm for applying RL to fusion control problems where high-fidelity simulators do not exist.

## 8. Conlin et al. (2024) — Differentiable Stellarator Optimization
Conlin et al. developed a spectrally accurate, reverse-mode differentiable bounce-averaging algorithm that enables efficient gradient-based optimization of stellarator coil geometries for minimizing neoclassical transport. The team demonstrated the first optimization of a finite-beta stellarator to directly reduce neoclassical ripple transport using reverse-mode differentiation, reducing optimization times from days to hours.

## 9. Padidar et al. (2025) — Diffusion for Stellarator Design
Padidar et al. proposed a conditional diffusion model trained on the QUASR database to rapidly generate high-quality quasisymmetric stellarator designs. Generated stellarators achieved less than 5% deviation from quasisymmetry and target characteristics, with the model demonstrating successful generalization to out-of-distribution target parameters. The generative model can sample new designs in seconds rather than the hours required by conventional optimization.
